The challenge

Parents and caregivers are a child's first and most constant reading teachers. Yet most guidance on how reading develops is written for educators, not families.

Reading Is Fundamental has 60 years of literacy expertise and an in-house team of reading specialists. The question was how to get that expertise to families where they already spend their time: on their phones, in the few minutes between everything else, and without an agency budget.

The approach

Two series, one trusted messenger

Both series star Karly O'Brien, RIF's Literacy Services Manager, a former PK–8 classroom teacher and reading specialist with a master's in Curriculum and Instruction. Each was designed for short-form social from the start, on TikTok and YouTube Shorts.

Anatomy of an Ask Karly episode

1Hook with a real question

Open with a question parents have asked themselves, so the episode feels relevant from the first second.

2Give a clear answer

Karly explains what the research says, in plain language.

3Leave a takeaway

End with one practical step families can use at home, grounded in how children learn to read.

Built to run without a budget

Every episode is produced in-house by RIF's marketing and literacy teams, which keeps donor dollars focused on getting books to children. The process is designed to be repeatable: about an hour per episode, six episodes a month.

Testing

We tested what made episodes work

From August 17 to September 25, 2026, I ran a head-to-head TikTok test: 18 posts in 9 matched pairs, comparing RIF's three recurring video series.

Question-led hooks and named phonics skills beat broad themes. "Do audiobooks count?" and "Same book on repeat?" became the top two TikTok posts of the quarter. The test also showed what didn't matter: a branded shirt versus everyday clothes made no reliable difference, so wardrobe won't drive production decisions.

The result shaped the calendar: more Ask Karly, and Book Recs reworked around timely hooks.

The result

Families watched, saved, and came back

On TikTok, Ask Karly episodes average nearly twice the views of RIF's other posts and earn the highest like rate and watch time of any recurring RIF series. On YouTube, they average more than eight times the views of RIF's other 2026 videos.

Reading Fundamentals grew steadily through its first quarter. September episodes averaged more than four times the TikTok views of summer episodes. Viewers saved its lessons 52 times, a strong signal of usefulness, since people bookmark content they plan to come back to.

The series also changed RIF's YouTube channel. In the fourth quarter, 9 of the channel's top 10 videos were new that quarter, and Shorts grew from 36% to 56% of all views.

My role

I led the series for the marketing team alongside RIF's literacy experts: the content strategy and format, the publishing calendar, and the performance testing that decided what to make next.

I also designed and built the Webby Awards entry site that presents both series, from the copy and layout to the embedded episodes and results.

What it shows

A nonprofit doesn't need an agency to make video that works. A trusted in-house expert, a format built for the feed, and a habit of testing can outperform polished production.
