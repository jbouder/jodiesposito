Building systems that let small teams run

A leadership approach I've used at four organizations

Every organization I've worked for has had big goals and a small marketing team. My answer has been the same each time: I don't just do the work, I build the structure that lets the team do more of it, with less chaos and without burning out.

10 years

leading marketing teams at associations and nonprofits, since 2016

4

organizations where I've built how the marketing team plans, tracks, and reports

The challenge

Associations and nonprofits ask a lot of their marketing teams: websites, email, social, events, membership, advocacy, and reporting to leadership, usually with a handful of people and a tight budget.

Without structure, that work runs on memory, inboxes, and late nights. Deadlines slip, the same questions get asked twice, and the most capable people burn out first. Adding more effort doesn't fix it. Adding the right systems does.

My approach

Three principles I bring to every team

Make the work visible

One shared place for every project, owner, and deadline, so no one has to ask where things stand and nothing depends on one person's memory.

Do repeatable work once

Editorial calendars, recurring series formats, templates, and batched production turn one-off scrambles into routines the team can run.

Let data run the meeting

Regular, automated reporting replaces opinions with evidence, so the team spends its time on what works.

The result

Small teams, outsized output

The systems are how lean teams produced the results elsewhere on this site. At the Copyright Alliance, a small team grew creator membership 104%. At ILR, editorial calendars and an SEO guide for every writer helped grow organic traffic 9,000%. At RIF, a small team publishes across five social platforms, a newsletter, and a monthly book club, and grew social views 150% in a quarter.

What it shows

Leadership on a small team isn't about doing everything yourself. It's about building the structure that lets everyone do their best work: clear priorities, visible progress, routines that save time, and data that keeps the team focused.
